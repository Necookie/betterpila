# Content and Editorial Policy

**Status:** Draft for review  
**Last updated:** 2026-09-13

## 1. Editorial mission

Better Pila explains and organizes public-interest information about Pila, Laguna. It does not endorse candidates, parties, officials, contractors, or government programs.

Required public disclaimer:

> Better Pila is an independent civic transparency project and is not an official website of the Municipality of Pila.

## 2. Eligible content

- Elected and appointed officials acting in public roles.
- Municipal offices, services, official contacts, and hotlines.
- Budgets, expenditures, procurement, and audit material from lawful public sources.
- Public projects and programs.
- Ordinances, resolutions, and related public documents.
- Corrections and methodology notes relevant to the portal.

## 3. Prohibited or restricted content

- Rumors, unsourced allegations, partisan advocacy, or personal attacks.
- Private addresses, personal phone numbers, family information, identity numbers, signatures, or unrelated personal data.
- Emergency reports submitted with an expectation of official response.
- Copyrighted material republished without permission or a lawful basis.
- Automated summaries presented as verified human work.
- Speculation about motives, guilt, intent, or legality.

## 4. Source hierarchy

Prefer sources in this order, while evaluating each item on its merits:

1. Official records and publications from the Municipality of Pila.
2. National government sources such as COA, DBM, DILG, PhilGEPS, and official legal repositories.
3. Signed or formally issued records supplied by an accountable public office.
4. Reputable reporting that directly cites documents or named officials.
5. Secondary community sources used only as leads, not sole evidence for consequential claims.

An official social post may support a time-sensitive operational fact but should not replace a formal document when one exists.

## 5. Citation requirements

Each substantive published record must include:

- Source title and publisher.
- Direct source URL or a clearly identified document.
- Publication date when available.
- Date accessed or verified.
- An archival reference when lawful and practical.
- A note if the source is incomplete, inaccessible, superseded, or disputed.

Editors must not cite a search-results page when a direct source is available.

## 6. Writing style

- Use clear, factual, non-promotional language.
- Separate sourced facts from Better Pila's plain-language explanation.
- Define technical budget and legal terms.
- State what is unknown instead of inferring it.
- Use exact dates rather than relative phrases such as “recently.”
- Use Philippine peso formatting consistently.
- Avoid honorific inflation and language that favors or criticizes a person.

## 7. Summaries

A summary must accurately reflect the source, identify itself as a summary, and link to the full source. It must not omit a qualification that changes the meaning. Legal documents should include a notice that the summary is informational and the official text controls.

## 8. Corrections

- Provide a visible correction channel on every public record.
- Ask for the affected URL, claimed error, and supporting evidence.
- Never publish reporter identity without explicit, informed permission.
- Acknowledge requests within seven calendar days when possible.
- Correct clear errors promptly.
- Add a public correction note for material changes.
- Preserve the administrative revision history.
- Explain rejected correction requests internally and communicate the outcome when contact information exists.

## 9. Conflicts and disputed facts

When credible sources conflict:

- Do not choose silently.
- Describe the conflict neutrally.
- Cite each relevant primary source.
- Record the dates and context.
- Seek clarification from the responsible office when practical.
- Mark the record for follow-up review.

## 10. Images and documents

- Record creator, source, license/permission, and attribution.
- Prefer official or original files over screenshots.
- Remove unnecessary embedded personal information before lawful republication.
- Provide accessible text or an HTML summary for scanned PDFs when feasible.
- Never alter an official document in a way that could be mistaken for the original.

## 11. Artificial intelligence

AI tools may assist with transcription, extraction, translation, or draft summaries, but a human reviewer must compare the output with the source before publication. AI output is never itself a source.
